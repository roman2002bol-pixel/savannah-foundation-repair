# Niche research notes — why Tree Removal, Savannah GA

Kept for context on *why* the architecture is shaped the way it is, not as
something to re-litigate before every edit.

## The chosen niche

**Tree Removal, Savannah GA** — scored ~81/100 in a third-party niche
validator (CPC ~$17.91, ~580 monthly searches on core terms, no map pack
showing for the core term, organic results dominated by directories/small
sites rather than strong local competitors). Foundation Repair, Savannah GA
was the runner-up (~79/100, higher ticket price but weaker demand signal).

Standard caveat that applies to any such score: it's built from third-party
Google Ads/keyword data, which is never fully accurate and can make a
market look softer or busier than it is. The live SERP — actually searching
the term and studying who ranks — is the real test, not the score alone.

## The critical lesson: don't niche down into zero-volume exact phrases

Scoring the exact phrases "Emergency Tree Removal, Savannah GA" (56/100)
and "Storm-Damaged Tree Removal, Savannah GA" (43/100) separately produced
much worse scores than the broader "Tree Removal" term — because keyword
tools report ~0 searches for those exact phrases.

**That does not mean there's no demand for emergency/storm tree work.**
Someone with a tree on their roof after a storm doesn't type the tidy
phrase a keyword tool tracks — they search things like "tree fell on
house Savannah", "24/7 tree removal Savannah GA", "tree on my house",
"storm cleanup Savannah", "emergency chainsaw service". Keyword tools
under-report this kind of situational, panic-driven search behavior badly.

**The correct structure**: build the site around the broad, measurably-
demanded term (Tree Removal Savannah — real volume, real CPC, beatable
competition), and make the urgent/high-ticket variants **service pages
inside that site** (Emergency & Storm Tree Removal, Large/Hazardous Tree
Removal), not separate standalone niches or separate domains. This captures
both the broad commercial demand and the high-value urgent jobs, without
betting a whole site on a phrase with no measurable search volume.

This is the general principle behind the "don't make a zero-search-volume
long-tail its own site" rule in the main SKILL.md — it's not specific to
tree removal, it applies to any niche-down decision on a future microsite
in this folder.
